Expense Tracker App for the UAE: AED Spending, Cards and Bills

Money in the UAE moves in a particular rhythm: one salary in dirhams, rent in a few large cheques, a lot of card spending, and the occasional charge in another currency. A tracker that fits that rhythm is worth more than one with the longest feature list.

Quick answer

Pick a tracker that can read alerts from UAE banks so card and transfer spending shows up without typing, lets you set AED as the home currency, keeps rent and annual bills visible as commitments, and does not need your bank login. Then give it one full salary cycle before you judge your numbers.

The test: after a month, can you say what you spent in AED, what is already committed for next month, and which card each purchase went on?

What makes UAE spending different

Generic budgeting advice assumes rent is paid every month and most spending is cash or debit. In the UAE, a few things break that assumption:

  • Rent in cheques. Many leases are paid in one to four post-dated cheques a year. A AED 72,000 rent paid in four cheques is AED 18,000 leaving your account every three months, not AED 6,000 every month. If your tracker only sees the month the cheque clears, three months look rich and one looks like a disaster.
  • Cards everywhere. Groceries, fuel, food delivery and subscriptions usually go on a credit card, so spending and paying are two separate events. The purchase is the expense; paying the card bill later is a transfer.
  • Utility and telecom bills. DEWA, ADDC, SEWA or Etihad WE for power and water, plus an Etisalat (e&) or du bill, recur monthly but vary with the season. Summer power bills can be several times a winter one.
  • Charges in other currencies. Trips home, online shopping and app stores can bill in INR, USD, GBP or EUR. What matters for your budget is the AED amount that reached your card, including any foreign transaction fee.
  • VAT is already included. Shelf prices and receipts include 5% VAT, so you never need to add it when logging an amount.

How to record foreign-currency charges

When you pay INR 4,000 for a hotel in Kochi on a UAE card, the bank alert usually shows the AED amount debited, sometimes alongside the original amount. Log the AED figure as the expense, because that is what left your account. Put the original amount and currency in the note if you want to remember it.

Two things trip people up. First, the AED figure in the first alert can differ slightly from the statement, because the final rate is set when the merchant settles. Correct the amount if it changes by more than a few dirhams. Second, cards often add a foreign transaction fee, sometimes shown as a separate line. Treat it as part of the cost of that purchase, not as a mystery bank charge.

A 20-minute setup for your first salary cycle

  1. Set AED as the home currency.
  2. Add your rent as a recurring payment on each cheque date, with the full cheque amount.
  3. Add fixed monthly bills: phone, internet, gym, school transport, any car loan.
  4. Turn on automatic capture for your bank alerts (on iPhone this is a one-time Shortcuts setup; on Android it is notification access).
  5. Log cash spending by hand for one month. It is usually smaller than people expect, but taxis, tips and small shops add up.
  6. At month end, compare what you spent with your salary, then put a monthly amount aside for the next rent cheque.

Frequently asked questions

Does PayPilot work with UAE banks?

Automatic capture is built around the payment SMS banks send, from any bank in the UAE or India. If your bank wording is unusual, use Settings → Auto-capture → Test a bank message to see what would be logged.

Is there a free budget app for the UAE that does not need my bank login?

Yes. PayPilot's core features, including automatic capture of spending and budgets, are free and work from alerts on your phone rather than a bank connection.

Should I budget rent monthly or per cheque?

Both. Record each cheque on the date it clears, and set aside one twelfth of the annual rent every month so the cheque never lands on an empty account.

Can I track AED and INR in the same app?

Yes. Set AED as your home currency and record INR spending in INR when it happens in India. For a fuller two-country method, see the NRI guide.
