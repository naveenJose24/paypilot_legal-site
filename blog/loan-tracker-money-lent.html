How to Track Money Lent to Friends and Family (and Every Repayment)

Lending to friends and family rarely goes wrong because someone is dishonest. It goes wrong because nobody wrote anything down, three partial repayments later both people remember a different number, and a simple question becomes awkward.

Quick answer

Write down who, how much, when, and when it is due back, at the moment you lend. Record every repayment as its own entry, never by editing the original amount. Then the outstanding balance is always the original amount minus the repayments, and you can show the history if anyone asks.

What to record when you lend

FieldWhy
PersonObvious, but use the name you will search for later
Amount and currencyLending AED and being repaid in INR is common; note both
Date lentStarts the timeline
Expected repaymentA date, or "₹5,000 a month from March"
How it was paidBank transfer, UPI or cash. A transfer reference settles disputes
NoteWhat it was for. Months later this matters more than you think

A worked example with partial repayments

DateEntryOutstanding
5 JanLent ₹40,000 to Arun for a deposit₹40,000
2 FebRepaid ₹10,000₹30,000
6 MarRepaid ₹10,000₹20,000
1 MayRepaid ₹15,000₹5,000
3 JunRepaid ₹5,000Settled

If you had simply changed "₹40,000" to "₹20,000" in March, you would lose the history. Separate entries mean you can show Arun exactly what has been received, and he can check it against his own transfers.

Overpayments, forgiveness and money you borrowed

  • Overpaid? Record the repayment as received, then either return the extra or note it as credit towards something else. Do not let the balance go negative silently.
  • Forgiving the rest? Record a final entry such as "₹3,000 forgiven" so the loan closes cleanly and you are not reminded about it forever.
  • You borrowed? Track it the same way in the other direction. Knowing exactly what you owe is part of being easy to lend to.

Reminding someone without making it awkward

Agree on the repayment plan when you lend, ideally in a message, so a later reminder refers back to something you both said. When a payment is late, a short, factual note works best: "Hi, just checking on the ₹10,000 for March. I have ₹20,000 outstanding after the last one." The number is the reminder; you do not need to add pressure.

A formal loan with interest, or a large sum, is different. Put it in writing and consider professional advice. The EMI calculator can show what a fair monthly repayment looks like.

Frequently asked questions

Should I charge interest when lending to family?

That is a personal choice. If you do, agree the rate and repayment plan in writing at the start.

What if they repay in a different currency?

Record the repayment in the currency you received, and note the amount it covers in the loan's currency so the balance stays correct.

How do I close a loan I have decided to forgive?

Add a final entry for the forgiven amount. The loan closes with a full history showing what was repaid and what was written off.
