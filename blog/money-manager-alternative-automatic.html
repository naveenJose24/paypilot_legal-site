Money Manager, Wallet or a Spreadsheet? Choosing How to Track Expenses

There are four broad ways to track spending, and each one fails in a different way. The best one for you is the one you will still be using in six months, so it helps to know which failure you are most likely to hit.

Quick answer

If you enjoy building your own system, a spreadsheet gives the most control. If you are happy typing every expense, a manual ledger app like Money Manager is fast and simple. If you want automation and do not mind connecting your bank, a bank-sync app like Wallet covers more accounts. If you want automation without a bank connection, an alert-based tracker reads the payment alerts already on your phone.

When a spreadsheet is still the right answer

A spreadsheet is unbeatable if you want custom categories and formulas, if you are tracking something unusual (a shared house fund, a renovation, a wedding), or if you like the act of reviewing every line. Its weakness is capture: typing transactions in at the end of the week is the step people abandon. Some people keep both: an app for capture, and a monthly export into a spreadsheet for analysis.

If you like Money Manager but are tired of typing

Manual ledger apps are fast for each entry, but you still make every entry, and the ones you forget are usually small card and UPI payments. If that is your problem, the switch that helps is not more categories. It is capture: letting payment alerts create the records, and spending your attention on correcting a category now and then.

If you are weighing Wallet or another bank-sync app

Bank sync gives the widest coverage when your banks are supported, including accounts that do not send alerts. The trade-off is access: the app, or a provider it uses, holds a live connection to your accounts and full transaction history. If your banks are not supported, or you would rather not connect them, alert-based tracking gets most of the benefit with less access. Our privacy comparison goes into the differences.

How to switch without losing history

  1. Export your old data to CSV, if the app allows it, and keep the file.
  2. Start the new app on the first day of a month, or your next payday, rather than mid-cycle.
  3. Set up recurring payments first, then automatic capture.
  4. Run both side by side for one month if you want reassurance, then stop the old one.
  5. Do not try to re-enter years of history. A clean start with good records beats a perfect archive you never look at.

Frequently asked questions

Is PayPilot a good Money Manager alternative?

If your main frustration with Money Manager is typing every expense, yes: PayPilot creates records from payment alerts. If you prefer full manual control, a ledger app may still suit you better.

Can I move my data from another app?

Keep an export of your old data for reference. Starting fresh from a clear date is usually easier than re-entering history.

Is a spreadsheet better than an app?

It is more flexible, but harder to keep up to date. Many people use an app to capture spending and a spreadsheet for occasional analysis.
