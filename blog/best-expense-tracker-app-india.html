Expense Tracker App for India: UPI, Cards and Cash in One Place

In India the hard part is not big purchases. It is the twenty small UPI payments a day, split across two or three apps, plus cash that leaves no trail. A good tracker catches the digital ones automatically and makes the rest quick to add.

Quick answer

Use a tracker that reads your bank's debit SMS and, where SMS does not arrive, your bank or UPI app notifications. Log cash spending by hand, or treat ATM withdrawals as cash spending if you do not want the detail. Add EMIs and subscriptions as recurring payments. Then review once a week, not once a quarter.

Why UPI spending goes missing

UPI payments normally trigger a debit SMS from your bank, which is what makes automatic tracking possible. A few situations break that:

  • Small-payment SMS cut-offs. Some banks have stopped sending SMS for low-value UPI payments and send email instead. HDFC Bank, for example, stopped SMS alerts for UPI payments of ₹100 or less in 2024.
  • UPI Lite. Payments from the UPI Lite on-device wallet do not send an SMS each time. Your bank sends one summary SMS a day, and your statement only shows the top-up.
  • Wallet balances. Spending from a wallet balance (Paytm, Amazon Pay and similar) is not a bank debit, so your bank sends nothing.
  • Multiple apps. GPay, PhonePe, Paytm and your bank's own app can all pay from the same account. The bank SMS is the one thing they have in common.

The practical answer: let bank SMS do most of the work, add notification capture from your UPI apps if your bank skips small payments, and treat a UPI Lite or wallet top-up as one cash-like amount you spend down.

Handling cash without logging every rupee

You have two honest options. Log each cash spend as it happens, which gives the most detail. Or record the ATM withdrawal itself as a "cash" expense and stop there. The second is less precise, but your monthly total stays correct, and for most people the detail of where ₹2,000 of cash went is not worth the effort. Do not do both, or the same money is counted twice.

A simple first budget in INR

BucketWhat goes in it
FixedRent, EMIs, insurance premiums, phone and broadband, school fees
EverydayGroceries, fuel, food delivery, auto and cab rides, small UPI payments
OccasionalClothes, gadgets, travel, festivals and weddings
Future youSIPs, RD, emergency fund

Start by recording one month without changing anything. Then set a monthly limit for Everyday and Occasional based on what actually happened, not what you wish had happened. Pay Future you on salary day, before the Everyday money starts going.

EMIs and recurring debits

Loan EMIs, SIPs, insurance and OTT subscriptions are often auto-debited through an e-mandate. Under RBI rules your bank or card issuer must send a pre-debit notification before each recurring charge, which gives you a chance to stop one you no longer want. Add each one to your tracker as a recurring payment so the month's commitments are visible on day one. The EMI calculator shows how much of each EMI is interest.

What to compare when choosing an app

  • Capture: does it read SMS from your bank, and can it read UPI app notifications too?
  • Privacy: is the message read on your phone, or uploaded to a server? Does it ignore OTPs?
  • Corrections: can you fix a category or amount in one tap?
  • Duplicates: if both the SMS and the app notification arrive, does it log the payment once?
  • Cost: is automatic capture free, or locked behind a subscription?

Frequently asked questions

Why are my small UPI payments not being tracked?

Your bank may not send SMS for small UPI payments, or you may be paying from UPI Lite, which sends one daily summary instead. Add notification capture from your UPI app, or log those payments by hand.

Is there a free expense tracker for India that reads SMS automatically?

Yes. PayPilot's automatic capture of bank SMS is part of the free app.

Should ATM withdrawals count as spending?

Only if you are not also logging each cash purchase. Pick one method and stick with it so the money is not counted twice.

Does the app need my net banking password?

No. PayPilot works from the alerts your bank already sends to your phone.
