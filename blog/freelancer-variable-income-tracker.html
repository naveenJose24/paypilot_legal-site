Budgeting on Variable Income: A System for Freelancers

A salary budget assumes the same amount arrives on the same day every month. Freelance income does neither. The answer is not a more detailed budget. It is a buffer between the money that comes in and the money you live on.

Quick answer

Budget your personal life on your lowest normal month, not your average. Send every client payment into a holding account, set a share aside for tax, and pay yourself a fixed monthly amount from what remains. Good months fill the buffer; quiet months draw on it.

Step 1: find your baseline

List what you earned in each of the last 12 months, after business costs. Ignore the single worst month if it was a genuine one-off, such as illness or a move. The lowest remaining month is your baseline. Your fixed personal costs (rent, EMIs, insurance, groceries) need to fit inside it.

MonthEarned
Best monthAED 24,000
AverageAED 14,500
Lowest normal monthAED 9,000

Planning on the AED 14,500 average means four or five months a year where the money runs out. Planning on AED 9,000 means good months build a cushion instead of a lifestyle.

Step 2: pay yourself from a buffer

  1. Every client payment goes into one account that you do not spend from directly.
  2. Set the tax share aside first (see below).
  3. On the same day each month, move your fixed "salary" (your baseline) to your everyday account.
  4. When the buffer holds three months of salary, start moving the surplus to savings or investments.

The first few months are the hardest, because the buffer starts empty. A month or two of living below your baseline to build it is worth it.

Step 3: set tax aside as money arrives

Tax rules for freelancers differ by country and by your situation, so get advice for the details. The habit that works everywhere is to move a fixed percentage of every payment into a separate tax pot the day it arrives, so the money is there when the bill comes.

  • India: freelancers whose tax for the year exceeds the threshold usually pay advance tax in instalments during the year, due in June, September, December and March.
  • UAE: there is no personal income tax, but a freelancer operating under a business licence may fall under corporate tax and VAT registration rules depending on income. Check with an adviser.

Step 4: separate business and personal spending

Software, equipment, co-working, travel to clients and professional fees are business costs. Groceries are not, even if you eat them at your desk. Keeping them apart (a separate card helps) makes tax time easier and shows whether the business itself is profitable, which is a different question from whether you are saving money.

When a quiet month comes

If you have a buffer, a quiet month is a non-event: you pay yourself as usual. Without one, cut in this order: occasional spending first, then subscriptions, then everyday spending. Do not stop the tax pot, and avoid covering the gap with a credit card you cannot clear next month.

Frequently asked questions

How big should a freelancer's buffer be?

Three months of your personal salary is a common target. If your income is very seasonal, aim higher.

What percentage should I set aside for tax?

It depends on your income, country and deductions. Ask an adviser for a figure, then set that percentage aside from every payment.

Should I use a separate account for business?

It helps a lot. Even a second card used only for business costs makes tracking and tax much simpler.
