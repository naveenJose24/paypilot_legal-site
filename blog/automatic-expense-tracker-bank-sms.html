How Automatic Expense Tracking Works: Bank SMS, Notifications and Apple Pay

Automatic tracking is not magic and it is not a bank connection. It works by reading the payment alerts that already arrive on your phone. Knowing which alerts arrive, and which do not, tells you exactly what will be captured.

Quick answer

There are three sources: bank SMS, notifications from bank and UPI apps, and Apple Pay taps in shops. On iPhone each one is a Shortcuts automation you set up once. On Android, the app reads payment notifications after you grant notification access. Together they catch most card, UPI and transfer spending. Cash, some small UPI payments and wallet balances still need a quick manual entry.

Online Apple Pay payments are not lost. Your bank still sends an SMS or app notification for them, so the first two sources pick them up.

On iPhone: why it uses Shortcuts

iOS does not let apps read your messages. The only route is a Shortcuts personal automation that you create, which passes a matching message to the app. That limits what an app can see: only the texts that match your filter are handed over.

PayPilot's bank SMS automation uses the filter "Message contains ited", which matches debited, credited and deposited, so a single automation catches money going out and coming in. Messages with words like "limited" or "invited" also match. They are read on the phone, recognised as non-payments, and dropped without being saved. If your bank uses other wording, such as "spent" or "UPI debit:", add a second copy of the automation with that word. The auto-capture guide has screenshots for every step, and the iPhone SMS tracker page explains the setup in brief.

On Android: notification access

Android lets an app read notifications once you allow it in settings. With notification access on, PayPilot reads payment notifications from your SMS app, bank apps and UPI apps on the phone. It does not read chats or email. There is nothing to build: open Settings → Auto-capture in the app and tap Allow notification access.

What happens to a message once it is read

  1. The message is checked on the phone: is it a payment, and is it money in or out?
  2. OTPs, offers and messages without an account or payment reference are discarded.
  3. For a payment, the amount, merchant, date and last four card digits are extracted.
  4. If the same payment already arrived from another source (say SMS and Apple Pay), the repeat is skipped.
  5. The record is saved, and the message text itself is deleted. Nothing is sent to a server or an AI to read it.

Two genuinely different payments that look identical, like two coffees of the same price a minute apart, are both kept. If one is a mistake, delete it.

How to test it before you rely on it

In PayPilot, open Settings → Auto-capture → Test a bank message and paste a real SMS from your bank. It shows exactly what would be logged, or why not. After your next real payment you should get a notification such as "Auto-captured ₹250.00 · Swiggy". Tap it to check the category.

If nothing appears after a payment, check that the automation is on and Confirm Before Run is off, that the message contains your filter word, and then open the app once: items captured while it was closed are added when it opens.

What still needs a manual entry

  • Cash.
  • UPI Lite payments, which get one daily summary SMS instead of one per payment.
  • Spending from wallet balances such as Paytm or Amazon Pay.
  • Bank alerts that arrive on a SIM that is not in your phone.

For most people that is a handful of entries a week, against dozens captured automatically.

Frequently asked questions

Does automatic expense tracking need my bank login?

No. It reads the alerts your bank already sends to your phone. No bank credentials are involved.

Why was an online Apple Pay payment not captured by the Wallet automation?

iOS only runs Wallet automations for in-store taps. Online and in-app Apple Pay payments are captured from your bank's SMS or app notification instead.

Will a payment be logged twice if I get both an SMS and an app notification?

No. PayPilot recognises the repeat and keeps one record.

Does it work with Indian and UAE banks?

Yes. The bank SMS parser handles messages from banks in both countries. Use the in-app test to check your bank's wording.
