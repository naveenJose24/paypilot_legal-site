Subscription Tracker: Find, Audit and Cut Recurring Charges

Subscriptions are designed to be easy to start and easy to forget. Individually they look small. Together, and counted over a year, they are often one of the larger lines in a budget. A short audit, then a simple tracker, keeps them under control.

Quick answer

List every recurring charge from the last three months of card and bank statements, plus your App Store or Google Play subscriptions. Multiply each by 12 to see its yearly cost. Cancel anything you would not sign up for again today, and put the rest in a tracker with a reminder before each renewal.

Where subscriptions hide

  • App store billing. Subscriptions bought inside apps are billed by Apple or Google, so your statement says "Apple" or "Google", not the app's name.
  • Annual renewals. A yearly charge shows up once, so it never feels like a habit. It is the one most likely to surprise you.
  • Free trials. Most turn into paid plans automatically unless you cancel before the trial ends.
  • Bundles. Telecom plans and card benefits sometimes include a streaming service that you may also be paying for separately.
  • Family members. A service someone else in the house also pays for.

The 30-minute audit

  1. Open three months of card and bank statements and mark every charge that repeats.
  2. On iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions. On Android, open Google Play, tap your profile, then Payments & subscriptions.
  3. Write each one down with its price and how often it bills.
  4. Convert each to a yearly cost.
  5. For each, ask: did I use it in the last month, and would I sign up today at this price?
  6. Cancel or downgrade the ones that fail. Put the rest in your tracker.

"AED 35" sounds like nothing. "AED 420 a year for a service I opened twice" sounds like a decision. Seeing the yearly cost is usually enough to make the call.

Keep, pause or cancel

  • Keep what you use every week and would miss.
  • Rotate streaming services: keep one at a time and switch when you have finished what you wanted to watch.
  • Switch to yearly billing only for services you are certain to keep, and set a reminder before the renewal.
  • Cancel anything you are keeping "just in case". You can usually sign up again in a minute.

Recurring card charges in India and the UAE

In India, recurring card and UPI charges run on e-mandates, and RBI rules require a pre-debit notification before each charge. That notification is your last chance to cancel. Mandates can also be viewed and cancelled from your bank or UPI app. In the UAE, recurring charges are usually stored card payments; cancel with the merchant first, and if the charges continue, ask your bank about blocking them.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see all my subscriptions on iPhone?

Open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions. That lists everything billed through Apple. Subscriptions paid directly by card are on your card statement instead.

How do I cancel a subscription on Android?

Open Google Play, tap your profile picture, choose Payments & subscriptions, then Subscriptions, and cancel the one you no longer want.

Is monthly or yearly billing better?

Yearly is cheaper only if you would have kept the service all year. If you are not sure, stay monthly.
